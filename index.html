Why Kip

Shift work is hard on sleep

Normal sleep advice assumes you work nine to five. Rosters don't, and the hard bits are easy to miss until you're running on empty.

🌗

Your body clock fights you

Night and early shifts push sleep into hours your body wants to be awake, so the sleep you do get is often shorter and lighter.

Åkerstedt 2003, Occupational Medicine
↩️

Quick returns sneak up

A late followed by an early leaves a short gap once you count the trip home, winding down and getting ready, and that's when sleep gets cut.

Vedaa et al. 2016, Ergonomics
📉

Short sleeps add up

Losing an hour or two night after night builds a sleep debt that slows you down, often more than you notice yourself.

Van Dongen et al. 2003, Sleep

Kip reads your roster and plans around all of it, so you always know when to sleep, nap and stop the coffee.
